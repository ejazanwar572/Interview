# Behavioral and Leadership (Googleyness)

Google assesses non-technical skills heavily. They look for "Googleyness"—how you collaborate, navigate ambiguity, communicate, and push yourself out of your comfort zone.

## The STAR Method
Always structure your behavioral answers using the STAR format:
- **S - Situation**: Briefly set the context.
- **T - Task**: What was the specific problem or goal you were responsible for?
- **A - Action**: What specific steps did *you* take? Focus on your contributions, not just the team's.
- **R - Result**: What was the outcome? Use quantifiable metrics whenever possible. Explain what you learned.

## Key Themes and Example Questions

### 1. Navigating Ambiguity 
Google Data Scientists often work on vaguely defined problems. You must show how you bring structure to chaos.
- **Question**: "Tell me about a time you had to tackle a project with very vague requirements."
- **Prep Idea**: Think of a time you were given a broad goal (e.g., "improve retention") and how you broke it down into measurable steps, defined the metrics, and aligned stakeholders on the definition of success.

### 2. Failing and Learning
Google values leaders who can own their mistakes, conduct a root-cause analysis, and implement preventative measures.
- **Question**: "Tell me about a time when you failed to meet a deadline. What did you fail to do? What did you learn?" *(From Google Prep PDF)*
- **Prep Idea**: Do not give a fake flaw. Own a real mistake. 
  - *Root cause*: Did you underestimate the data quality issues? Did you fail to align with data engineering early enough?
  - *Lesson applied*: How did this change your workflow? (e.g., "I now implement a data validation sprint before committing to modeling timelines.")

### 3. Conflict Resolution and Stakeholder Management
As a Product DS, you will have to tell PMs and Engineers things they don't want to hear (e.g., "Your feature launch failed the A/B test").
- **Question**: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a Product Manager regarding a data insight or recommendation."
- **Prep Idea**: Focus on empathy and data-driven communication. How did you validate their concerns while standing your ground on the statistical rigor? How did you compromise or find a path forward (e.g., "Let's run the test for one more week to reach sufficient power")?

### 4. Leadership Without Authority
You might not manage people directly, but you manage projects and directions.
- **Question**: "Describe how you have used your communication and decision-making skills to mobilize others." *(From Google Prep PDF)*
- **Prep Idea**: Focus on a time you identified a gap, proposed a solution, and convinced cross-functional partners (Engineers, PMs, Ops) to dedicate resources to your idea based on data you surfaced.

## Quick Tips for Googleyness
- **Be Collaborative**: Say "I" when talking about your actions, but praise the "Team" when talking about the broader success.
- **Be Intellectually Humble**: It's completely fine to say, "In hindsight, I would have done X differently because..."
- **Be User-Centric**: Always bring the conversation back to how an internal decision ultimately affected the end user's experience.
